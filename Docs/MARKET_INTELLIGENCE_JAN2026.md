## Executive Summary

Soybean oil (ZL) enters 2026 in a complex equilibrium: **bearish fundamentals** (record global supply, high ending stocks) versus **bullish policy drivers** (EPA RFS mandates, 45Z tax credits, foreign feedstock penalties). The balance tips toward **stable-to-slightly-bullish ZL prices** through H1 2026, driven by biodiesel/renewable diesel demand absorbing excess supply.

**Key Takeaways:**
- ZL price forecast: **$0.53/lb** (USDA ERS, unchanged from December)
- US soybean oil use in biomass-based diesel: **15.5 billion lbs** (+3.7B from MY 2024/25)
- Brazil soybean harvest: **Record 178 MMT** (bearish global supply)
- EPA BBD RVOs: **+32.8% for 2026** (bullish domestic demand)
- Tariff suspensions expire: **Nov 10 & Dec 31, 2026** (event risk)

## 2. Biofuel Policy Landscape

### 2.1 EPA Renewable Fuel Standard (RFS) 2026-2027

On June 13, 2025, EPA proposed new RFS volumes representing the most significant regulatory development for biomass-based diesel in the program's history.

**Volume Requirements (RINs, billions):**

| Category | 2025 | 2026 | 2027 | Change 2025→2026 |
|----------|------|------|------|------------------|
| **Biomass-Based Diesel** | 5.36 | 7.12 | 7.50 | **+32.8%** |
| **Advanced Biofuel** | 4.98 | 7.35 | 7.67 | +47.6% |
| **Cellulosic Biofuel** | 1.19 | 1.30 | 1.36 | +9.2% |
| **Total Renewable Fuel** | 22.33 | 24.02 | 24.46 | +7.6% |

**Key Policy Changes:**

1. **Foreign Feedstock Penalty (Critical for ZL):**
   - Starting 2026, imported renewable fuel or fuel from foreign feedstocks generates **only 50% of RINs**
   - In 2024, ~45% of BBD feedstock came from foreign sources
   - Creates structural advantage for domestic soybean oil

2. **eRINs Removed:**
   - Renewable electricity no longer qualifies under RFS
   - Removes competing pathway for RIN generation

3. **Small Refinery Exemptions:**
   - More restrictive criteria
   - Mandatory reallocation if granted

**Projected Impact:**
> "Revised BBD RVOs look to increase around 50% over 2026-2027 compared to 2023-2025." — farmdoc daily

### 2.2 Section 45Z Clean Fuel Production Tax Credit

The 45Z tax credit consolidates and replaces pre-IRA biofuel credits, basing eligibility on carbon intensity rather than fuel type.

**Current Structure:**
- Base credit: Up to **$1.75/gallon** for low-CI fuels
- SAF credit: Reduced to **$1.00/gallon** under OBBBA (July 2025)
- Estimated program cost: **$65.5 billion** through 2034

**Key Changes Under "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" (OBBBA):**

1. **ILUC Exclusion:**
   - Indirect land use change emissions excluded from carbon intensity scoring
   - Benefits corn ethanol and **soy biodiesel** by 20-25 g CO2e/MJ
   - Results in higher per-gallon tax credits

2. **Feedstock Restrictions:**
   - Starting 2026, feedstocks must be sourced from **US, Canada, or Mexico**
   - Reinforces domestic soybean oil demand

3. **Extended Timeline:**
   - Credit extended through **2029** (was 2027)

**Implications for ZL:**
- More than 50% of US soybean oil expected to go into biofuels
- ILUC exclusion makes soy biodiesel more competitive vs imported feedstocks
- Combined with RFS foreign penalty, creates strong domestic demand floor

### 2.3 California Low Carbon Fuel Standard (LCFS)

**Current Credit Prices:**
- Spot price: **$40-50/tonne** (after July 2025 amendments)
- Historical peak: ~$230/tonne (2022)
- Historical low: $40.25 (June 2025)

**2025 Regulatory Amendments (Effective July 1, 2025):**
- Addresses credit bank oversupply (>40M credits in Q1 2025)
- Tightens credit supply to support prices
- Expected to provide floor under LCFS values

**Renewable Diesel's Role:**
- RD accounts for ~81% of LCFS credits generated (Q3 2023)
- 50% of California's diesel demand now met by biofuel alternatives
- Soybean oil is primary RD feedstock

## 3. China Trade Dynamics

### 3.1 Current Trade Deal Status

**US-China Economic and Trade Arrangement (November 2025):**

| Commitment | Details | Expiration |
|------------|---------|------------|
| China ag tariff suspension | All retaliatory tariffs on US ag suspended | **Dec 31, 2026** |
| US reciprocal tariff suspension | Heightened tariffs on China suspended | **Nov 10, 2026** |
| Soybean purchase commitment | ≥25 MMT annually (2026-2028) | 2028 |
| China export controls | Rare earth restrictions suspended | Nov 10, 2026 |

**Context:**
- 25 MMT commitment is **14% below** the 2020-2024 five-year average
- US ag exports to China: **$9B forecast for 2026** (lowest since 2018 trade war)
- 88% of ag economists believe exports will NOT return to pre-trade war levels

### 3.2 Structural Shift to Brazil

**China's Diversification Strategy:**
- Soybean imports from Brazil: **114 MMT** (record)
- Soybean imports from US: ~25 MMT (committed minimum)
- Infrastructure investments in Latin America: Billions in ports, roads, storage

**Key Insight:**
> "Once foreign buyers reorient toward alternative suppliers, markets are slow to recover. Chinese importers are deepening partnerships with Brazil and Argentina." — American Farm Bureau Federation

**Implications:**
- US soybean exports structurally lower regardless of tariff policy
- Domestic crush demand (for biodiesel) becomes more critical for ZL pricing
- Brazil weather risk remains relevant (La Niña monitoring)

### 3.3 Tariff Deadline Risk

**Critical Dates:**
- **November 10, 2026:** Section 301 reciprocal tariff suspension expires
- **December 31, 2026:** China agricultural tariff suspension expires

**Event Risk:**
- Market repricing likely intensifies 60-90 days before expiration
- SCOTUS ruling on IEEPA tariff authority expected early 2026
- If tariffs reimposed: Bullish US domestic crush, bearish exports
